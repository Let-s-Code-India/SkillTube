Badges and ticks you cannot buy

SkillTube awards badges through published activity rules; audience ticks are not paid verification.

2026-11-24
badgestrust

Badges can be useful when their rules are visible and they cannot be bought. SkillTube awards badges from activity and milestones recorded by the service, such as early views, followers, cross-country reach or a weekly ranking. The badge catalog explains how each award is earned, so a visitor can read the rule instead of guessing what an icon means.

The awards have no cash value and are not a guarantee that a resource is secure, accurate or suitable for a task. They describe a particular milestone, not an independent audit. A badge can also be revoked when its underlying conditions no longer apply or when an associated listing is removed after a report.

Ticks have a different meaning. The Blue Tick corresponds to an audience milestone of one million followers; it is not paid verification and does not prove a developer's identity. The Master Tick reflects a threshold involving Blue-Tick followers from multiple countries. Those signals describe reach, not code quality or endorsement by SkillTube.

Keeping the criteria public helps developers understand what the product measures and helps visitors interpret what they see. The rules are implemented in backend functions and can change as the product evolves. If the criteria change, the catalog should continue to explain the current requirement rather than relying on a badge name alone.

Badges also encourage some forms of participation, but they should not encourage spam or artificial engagement. SkillTube's rules prohibit fake views, stars and follows. The service uses counters and scheduled ranking jobs to evaluate awards, while moderation can respond to abuse.

When you see a badge, treat it as one small piece of context. Read the earning rule, then inspect the listing and its repository for your own needs. The purpose of a badge is to make a defined activity milestone visible, not to ask you to trust a project without review.

Developers should use badges as a record of the milestone, not as marketing language that promises a result. A visitor should be able to open the catalog and see the rule in plain words. If an award is revoked, its status should be clear rather than presented as current. This makes the visual system more honest and reduces the chance that an achievement is mistaken for a security certification.
