Free for developers and users, with no credit card

SkillTube is designed around open-source software and free service plans, with no paid APIs or subscriptions.

2026-11-24
freeopen source

SkillTube is built to be free for people browsing and for developers publishing. There is no paid tier, subscription or credit-card step in the product. The code is open source, and its current hosting and database setup uses free service plans. That is a design intention, not a promise that every provider will keep the same limits or terms forever.

Avoiding a paid API also keeps the product simpler to inspect. Public discovery uses documented functions, the website code is available in the repository, and the catalog runs through an automated workflow. Contributors can see how pages and listings are assembled instead of depending on a proprietary discovery service whose rules cannot be examined.

Free does not mean costless to operate. Hosting providers have quotas, and database usage can grow as listings and activity increase. Free plans may change, and a service can become temporarily unavailable or need a different approach. SkillTube aims to stay within free tiers and open-source tooling, but it should be honest about these practical limits rather than implying unlimited capacity.

The product also avoids using ads or tracking analytics to fund itself. That choice means there is no advertising profile or paid placement in the browsing experience. Visibility comes from directory search and product activity, not from a developer buying a promotion. This does not make every ranking neutral in every possible way; the ranking rules and their limits should remain visible.

A free service still needs clear policies. The privacy, community and acceptable-use pages explain what the service stores and how reports are handled. The project is for adults, and GitHub account eligibility and repository ownership checks apply to publishing. These requirements are part of responsible operation, not a hidden paid gate.

If you use SkillTube, you can browse public pages without paying or moving your repository. If you contribute, you can inspect the code and help improve it. We will keep describing the service as it exists, including its provider limits, rather than selling a guarantee of permanent availability.

There is no checkout flow for publishing, no fee to appear in search and no subscription that unlocks ordinary discovery features. That matters for a directory because paid placement can change what visitors see. SkillTube aims to keep listings discoverable through relevant information and its ranking rules. The project still needs to monitor usage and service-provider terms, and it should tell users plainly if those operating conditions change.
