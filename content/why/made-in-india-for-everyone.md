SkillTube is built by Let's Code India, an open-source organisation based in Mumbai, Maharashtra. The project is made in India and is intended for developers and visitors around the world. The location describes who operates the service; it does not limit who can browse public pages or publish an eligible AI resource.

The service uses a simple model: developers keep their public repositories on GitHub, while SkillTube provides a directory, structured listing pages and developer channels. Contributors can inspect the source code, documentation and policies. Users can read the rules and see how publishing, reports and badges are meant to work.

Being open source makes participation possible, but it does not mean the project is run by a large company or has unlimited support capacity. The service relies on free plans and volunteer effort. Features may change, providers may have outages, and support is handled through the published contacts. The team should be transparent about those limits.

The directory welcomes different kinds of AI-related work. AI skills remain the main focus, but MCP servers, datasets, databases, prompts, agents, workflows and tools can also be listed when they live in a public repository. A shared format helps visitors discover work across communities without requiring every developer to use the same framework.

The project's legal pages identify the operator, contacts and grievance process. Indian law governs the service, and privacy handling is described in the Privacy Policy. Copyright and security concerns have dedicated paths so reports can reach the right people.

If you build an AI resource, you can share it through a listing and your developer channel. If you use one, review its repository and licence first. The goal is a useful community layer that is built locally, accessible globally and open to contribution.

Operating from India means the legal pages identify Indian law and Mumbai as the jurisdiction, while the directory remains open to developers in other places. The service's country field is private and is used only for aggregate counts and badge rules. It is not shown on public pages. The project aims to make that distinction clear so a global audience can participate without confusing location metrics with a public profile field.
